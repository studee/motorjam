MotorJam Racer – Privacy Policy

Effective November 13, 2026 (your phone's language setting added; first-steps counts added; November 10: TikTok ad measurement added, made for players 13 and up)

MotorJam Racer is a game for Android, made for players aged 13 and up. This policy explains what happens to your information when you play it.

What we collect

Very little, and never anything that names you. There are no ads in the game, and we never sell anything. Single-player needs no internet. If you sign in with Google Play Games (below), our service keeps your Play Games player ID with your online points and a copy of your game progress. We advertise the game on TikTok, and the app tells TikTok a few things so we can see whether those ads work (TikTok ad measurement, below).

Google Play Games sign-in

The game signs in to Google Play Games so your progress is safe and online races are fair. Single-player works without it.

Playing as a guest. If you don't sign in to Google Play Games (or the sign-in doesn't work), you can still race online as a guest: the game makes a random code on your phone and keeps it there, and our service keeps your guest progress (the same things as below) under a scrambled form of that code. It says nothing about you or your phone. If you later sign in with Google Play Games on a new account, your guest progress moves to it. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data loses the guest code, and with it the guest progress.

  • What we keep. Our service (on Cloudflare) keeps, under your Google Play Games player ID: your online points, the results of your online races (who won each race), a copy of your game progress (stars, coins, records, unlocked roads and bikes, purchases) and your phone's language setting (for example "Portuguese (Brazil)"), so we can see which languages the game needs. It's the setting you chose on your phone, not where you are: we don't collect your location. We don't keep your name, your email, your Play Games display name, or the nickname you type for online races.
  • Why. So a reinstall or a new phone gets your progress back, and so online points can't be faked: our service, not the phone, decides each online race and keeps the count.
  • How. When you sign in, the game sends our service a one-time code from Google, which our service exchanges with Google for your Play Games player ID. Google processes the sign-in under its own privacy policy.
  • How long. Until you ask us to delete it: see Deleting your data.

Online races

If you choose ONLINE to race another player, the game connects to the internet, and only then:

  • Matchmaking. Your phone connects to our matchmaking service (hosted on Cloudflare) to be paired with another rider, either by quick match or with a room code. Like any internet connection, this reveals your IP address to the service. It is used only to pair you (riders on the same network are paired first), to place the connection near you, and to protect the service from abuse (rate limits). It is kept in memory only while you are being matched and is not stored or logged by us. As our hosting provider, Cloudflare processes connection data under its own privacy policy.
  • The race. Once paired, your phone connects directly to the other rider's phone, so each phone sees the other's IP address. If a direct connection isn't possible, the race data goes through a relay instead: a TURN relay service (ExpressTURN) or our own relay on Cloudflare. A relay only passes the race messages between the two phones and keeps nothing. What's exchanged: the bike you ride, its position and speed during the race, and your finish time. No names, no chat, no photos, no accounts.
  • Counts, not people. The matchmaking service keeps anonymous counts so we can see whether online play works and stays within its limits: how many races were matched, how long riders waited, how races connected, and how many requests were refused. These records hold no IP address, no device identifier and nothing else that could identify a player.
  • First steps. While the game is new on a phone, it tells our service (on Cloudflare), once each, when it reaches its first steps: the first launch, the training (started, finished or skipped), the first practice race (started, finished), the sign-in to Google Play Games (tried, worked, failed) and the first online race, with the app's version and the phone's language setting. Nothing else is sent: no name, no player ID, no device identifier. Like any request it reveals your IP address to the service for that moment only; it is not stored. We only see how many new installs reached each step, so we can tell where new players get stuck.
  • Finding the service. If the game can't reach the matchmaking service, it reads a small settings file from our website (hosted on GitHub Pages) that says where the service is now. That request, like any web request, reveals your IP address to GitHub; nothing else is sent.
  • Your nickname. The name you type on the ONLINE page is sent to the other rider for that race (it's shown over your bike). It's kept on your phone, not by us.
  • Results. Both phones tell our service who won; it decides the race and changes the two riders' online points (see Google Play Games sign-in, above).

TikTok ad measurement

We advertise MotorJam Racer on TikTok. To know which ads bring players, the app includes TikTok's measurement code (the TikTok Business SDK). It sends TikTok:

  • App events: that the app was installed and opened, that you finished your first race, and when you buy a gem pack (which pack, its price and currency, never your payment details).
  • Device information: your phone's advertising ID (the resettable ID Android gives apps for ad measurement), the phone model, the Android and app versions, language, and your IP address.

TikTok uses this to tell whether you came from one of our ads and to show our ads to people more likely to enjoy the game, under its own privacy policy. We see only totals (how many installs and purchases came from an ad), never you. You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in Android's settings (Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Security & privacy > Other privacy settings > Ads); with it deleted, these events can't be linked to you. Data TikTok holds is deleted through TikTok (see its policy); we can't delete it for you.

Freeze and crash reports

If the game freezes for more than about a second during a race, hits an error in its code, or closes unexpectedly in the middle of a race, it sends our service (on Cloudflare) a short report (after the race, or the next time the game starts), so we can find and fix what caused it. An error report holds the error message and where in the game's code it happened. A report holds the phone's model, its graphics chip, the Android and app versions, and numbers about the game at that moment (how long the freeze lasted, the frame rate, memory use, the road and speed, and what had just happened in the race, such as a crash). It holds no name, no account, no player ID and nothing you typed. Like any request it reveals your IP address to the service for that moment only; it is not stored. The reports are kept in our service's logs for 3 days, and as anonymous counts by phone model.

In-app purchases

The shop sells optional packs of gems. The whole game can be played without buying anything.

  • Google Play handles the payment. You pay through Google Play; we never see your name, email, card or any payment details. Google processes the purchase under its own privacy policy.
  • Checking a purchase. To make sure a purchase is real before adding it to your game, the game sends our service (on Cloudflare) the purchase token Google gives that purchase and the name of the item. Our service asks Google whether that purchase is complete, answers yes or no, and keeps nothing: no token, no order number, nothing about you or your phone. Like any web request, it reveals your IP address to the service for that moment only.
  • What you bought is recorded in your game's save on your phone (and, signed in to Google Play Games, in the copy of your progress our service keeps).

Unlock codes

We may give someone a personal code that unlocks every road and bike. Redeeming it sends only the code to our service (on Cloudflare), which checks it and counts how many times it has been used. Nothing else about you or your phone is sent or kept.

What stays on your device

Your game progress and settings (stars, coins, unlocked roads and bikes, purchases, best times, and options such as the music volume) are saved in the app's private storage on your phone. Settings never leave the device. When you're signed in to Google Play Games, a copy of your progress is also kept by our service (above).

Permissions

The game uses vibration for haptic feedback, and may read the phone's motion sensors if you choose tilt steering. Sensor readings are used only while you play and are not stored or sent anywhere. It uses the internet only for Google Play Games sign-in and its copy of your progress, online races, checking purchases and unlock codes, freeze and crash reports, and TikTok ad measurement (above). For the TikTok measurement it has Android's advertising ID permission (AD_ID).

Age

MotorJam Racer is made for players aged 13 and up. It is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect anything from them. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game online, contact us at the address below and we'll delete that player's data (see Deleting your data).
